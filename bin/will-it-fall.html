Model: each step is derived in the accompanying document. Radiator net rejection is two-face emission minus the Sun load and the Earth infrared load from a numerically integrated view factor. Ram area is the projected area of arrays, radiators, and bus. Propellant is drag force times time divided by g₀ Isp, which does not depend on spacecraft mass.

Not modeled: albedo, Earth infrared pressure, thermal-emission thrust, attitude torques, geomagnetic storms, and density changes within a solar cycle beyond the F10.7 setting. Density is NRLMSIS 2.1 averaged over a dawn-dusk orbit. Radiation pressure treats the array as absorbing with a 1.10 coefficient and the radiator as diffuse with 1.60. The drag coefficient of a near-edge-on plate is not really constant. Deorbit time assumes the attitude is held, whereas a real failure could leave the craft tumbling, and it uses the same orbit-mean density, so treat it as an order of magnitude. Mass assumptions are placeholders to be checked.

Production figures: zinc 13.0 Mt (2025 estimate) and bismuth 16,000 t (2025 estimate) from the USGS zinc and USGS bismuth summaries. Xenon about 50 t per year, within the 40 to 60 t quoted by Symmetry and Scientific Update. Krypton about 300 t per year is weakly sourced: I converted a market report's 75,000 to 90,000 m³ per year at 3.75 kg/m³. Argon about 9 Mt per year converts 5.1 billion m³ from an IndexBox summary at 1.78 kg/m³. Replace any of these in the panel.
